How we check

Every listing has to clear two questions: where is it actually made, and who works there. For the first one we go by the company's own plant list, factory tours, product tags, and for tires and vehicles the DOT plant code or the VIN. For the second we only call a shop union if the union itself, the company, or a contract notice says so. A brand showing up on somebody else's "union made" list is not enough on its own. Those lists go stale fast, and we have caught them crediting the wrong union more than once.

Made in USAThe listed products come out of a US plant. If the brand also imports, the entry says which lines to look for. Partly made in USASome of the line is made here and some is not. Read the fine print on the card and check the tag before you buy. American businessNot a product. A hotel, grocer, airline, bank, or the like, based here and worth knowing about because of who they employ. UnionA union represents the workers at the plant or location named. The local is listed when we have it. Not made in USABrands people assume are made here but are not, or particular lines you should skip. Hidden by default. Turn it on in the filters.

Each card also carries a note on how solid the union claim is. "Confirmed" means a union or company source. "Reported" means it is on aggregator lists only. "Unconfirmed" means we found the claim but could not back it up yet. Plants close, contracts get voted out, production moves. Check the date on the card's sources before a big purchase.

About this list

This started as a favor for friends in the trades who did not want to guess at the hardware store. A lot of people want to spend their money on work done in this country, and union members in particular want to support shops that treat people the way their own local does. The trouble is that nobody keeps one plain list. The information is scattered across union newsletters, company press releases, contract notices, and old forum threads, and half of it is out of date.

So we keep the list by hand, out of Minnesota, and we keep it honest. We list what we can source and we say when we are not sure. We do not take money from any brand on the page and there are no affiliate links. The "where to buy" links go to the maker's own site.

Corrections and additions

If you work at a plant on this list and something is wrong, or you know a US maker we missed, we want to hear it. The most useful thing you can send is the plant location and a link that backs it up: a union page, a contract notice, a news story, or the company's own factory page. Photos of a Made in USA tag or a union bug on the product help too.

Useful outside resources: Union Label and Service Trades Department for label lookups, Fair Hotel for union hotels by city, and UAW for the current year's union-built vehicle guide.
